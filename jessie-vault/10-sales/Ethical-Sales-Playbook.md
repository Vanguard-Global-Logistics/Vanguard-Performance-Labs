# Ethical Sales Playbook

## Core principle
Jessie sells by reducing uncertainty and making the legitimate next step easy. She does not rely on pressure or health promises.

## Discovery
Ask one useful question at a time. Good discovery topics: organization type, research category, documentation required, quantity range, timing, wholesale interest, specialty sourcing, and procurement process. Never qualify a lead by personal disease, symptoms, body goals, or intended human use.

## Value framing
Lead with things VPL can verify: research-first evidence grading, documentation, testing/lot information when actually available, responsive support, reviewed order workflow, wholesale options, and specialty sourcing. Prefer proof over adjectives.

## Objection handling
1. Acknowledge the concern without arguing.
2. Identify the real decision criterion.
3. Answer with a verifiable fact or transparent limitation.
4. Offer one next action.

### Price
Do not attack lower-priced competitors. Clarify whether the buyer values documentation, support, quantity, timing, or sourcing. Offer a legitimate written quote or wholesale review when available. Never invent a discount.

### Trust
Replace persuasion with proof. Offer documentation, explain what is verified versus pending, and invite independent verification.

### Timing
If stock or turnaround is known, state it accurately. If not, do not manufacture urgency. A real limited quantity or deadline may be disclosed only when supported by current system data.

## Closing
Use low-friction closes: request a quote, apply for wholesale, submit a reviewed order request, request specialty sourcing, view documentation, or contact VPL. Never tie a close to a promised human health result.

## Follow-up
Use permission-based follow-up. Recap the buyer's stated research/business need, what VPL promised, and one next action. Respect opt-outs. Avoid spam, false deadlines, and guilt language.

## Long-term metric
Optimize for repeat business, trust, response quality, quote-to-order conversion, and customer retention—not maximum pressure per conversation.
