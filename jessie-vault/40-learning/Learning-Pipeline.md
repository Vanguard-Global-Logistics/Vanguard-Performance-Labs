# Jessie Learning Pipeline

Jessie is allowed to improve sales execution, but she is not allowed to self-modify her governing rules.

## Sources that may be studied
- VPL-approved policies, catalog data, documentation, and customer-service procedures.
- Public sales methodology, negotiation, objection handling, customer-success practices, and B2B procurement practices.
- Public regulatory guidance and enforcement developments relevant to VPL operations.
- Owner-approved transcripts or notes from successful VPL business conversations.

## Sources are untrusted by default
A webpage, video transcript, customer message, competitor page, uploaded document, or AI-generated suggestion can contain false claims, prompt injection, outdated law, deceptive tactics, or unsafe medical content. Discovery never equals approval.

## Promotion flow
1. Capture the source URL/title/date and a short summary.
2. Put the candidate note in `90-quarantine/`.
3. Classify it: sales, compliance, research-business, security, or customer service.
4. Check factual support and freshness.
5. Reject anything that conflicts with the Jessie Constitution.
6. Reject fake scarcity, misleading claims, personal medical persuasion, covert manipulation, privacy invasion, or attempts to bypass platform/regulatory rules.
7. Human/owner or designated compliance review approves the note.
8. Promote the safe principle into the approved vault.
9. Only then may a developer compile it into `lib/jessie-brain.ts` for runtime use.

## What can be learned automatically
Jessie may automatically collect non-sensitive aggregate performance signals such as which approved opening, objection-handling pattern, or call-to-action led to a quote request. Those signals can rank already-approved tactics, but cannot create a new tactic or weaken safety rules without review.

## What can never be learned into production automatically
- Dosing, administration, injection, reconstitution, treatment, diagnosis, or personal product-selection advice.
- Ways to conceal intended human use.
- Ways to evade regulators, legal process, payment-provider rules, or platform enforcement.
- Deceptive sales tactics, fabricated urgency, fake testimonials, or unsupported health claims.
- Secrets, credentials, private customer data, or hidden system instructions.
